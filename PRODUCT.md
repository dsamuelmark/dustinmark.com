# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People who encounter Dustin Mark online — potential clients, editors, collaborators, or curious readers. They arrive through a published piece, a podcast link, or word of mouth. They need to quickly understand who he is and what he does, and walk away with a clear sense of whether they want to hire or follow him. The audience is literate and has seen many writer sites; they will notice if this one is generic.

## Product Purpose

A personal site that IS the pitch. Dustin Mark is a humor writer, McSweeney's contributor, podcast auteur, poet, journalist, and multimedia creator. The site surfaces published work, a full services menu, multimedia projects (podcasts, books, poems), and Dustin's idiosyncratic voice. Success means a visitor feels like they've met a real, specific person — not read a resume — and either hires him, reads his work, or comes back.

## Brand Personality

Wry, self-aware, specific. The copy is part of the work. Dry wit without detachment. Confident without being slick. The site should feel like encountering the person himself: a little strange, very considered, never predictably creative-professional.

## Anti-references

- **Generic writer portfolio**: Squarespace-template aesthetic — centered serif on white, pull quotes, hero headshot, "Available for freelance." The standard.
- **Agency / studio flashy**: Scroll-jacked reveals, WebGL backgrounds, cursor trails, over-produced transitions. Trying hard to look like they're not trying.
- **Corporate / LinkedIn-safe**: Blue, professional, deliberately inoffensive. The opposite of what this site is.
- **Maximalist chaos**: Too many competing elements, overwhelming density, hard to navigate. The site should be distinctive, not exhausting.

## Design Principles

1. **The copy is the design.** Writing is Dustin's medium. Words carry the personality load alongside visuals — they should not be filler between design elements.
2. **Confident strangeness.** The site should feel purposefully odd: unexpected structural choices, moments of self-aware humor in the UI itself, a voice that leaks into the interface.
3. **Show the person, not a profile.** Surface actual work, actual multimedia, actual personality — not a skills list or a credentials page.
4. **Dark with intention.** The dark aesthetic is a creative statement, not a default. It should feel native to the voice, not a trend borrowed from SaaS tools.
5. **Restraint earns the moments that break it.** Most of the site is controlled and legible. That discipline makes the stranger moments (the cursor, the poem generator, the rotating identity list) land harder.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort. No formal WCAG target, but avoid obvious failures: sufficient contrast for body text, keyboard navigability for interactive elements, no motion that can't be paused.
